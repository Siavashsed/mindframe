**Answer this question:** What are the major changes in the DSM-5-TR compared to the original DSM-5?

The DSM-5-TR (Text Revision), released in March 2022, was more modest than many expected. Here's what actually shifted:

**New Diagnosis**: One entirely new condition was added—this represents rare movement in diagnostic manuals, where the bar is intentionally high.

**Language Updates**: About a dozen existing diagnoses received updated language and clarifications, reflecting newer research and clinical experience since 2013. These weren't fundamental reconceptualizations, but refinements in how conditions are described and understood.

**Suicide Risk Coding**: The APA finally incorporated suicide risk severity coding that clinicians had been requesting for years. This addresses a real clinical need—the ability to code and track suicide risk systematically.

**What Didn't Change**: The structure and core framework remained intact. There was no wholesale reorganization of diagnostic categories, which disappointed some who hoped for deeper revisions.

The interesting part is *why* the changes were conservative. The field is split: some see this as appropriately cautious—we shouldn't overhaul diagnostic categories without overwhelming evidence. Others view it as timid, arguing we missed opportunities to address documented gaps.
