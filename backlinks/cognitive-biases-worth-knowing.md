## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do intelligent people still make irrational decisions despite understanding cognitive biases?

This is one of the most persistent paradoxes in cognitive psychology, and I've observed it countless times across different domains—finance, medicine, law, even among researchers studying bias itself.

The core issue is that understanding cognitive biases intellectually doesn't protect you from them. Your brain operates on two systems: System 1 (fast, intuitive, automatic) makes most decisions, while System 2 (slow, deliberate, logical) shows up afterward to rationalize what System 1 already decided. Intelligence actually makes this worse because intelligent people are better at constructing convincing narratives to defend biased choices.

I watched this unfold in a study with experienced financial advisors. These were experts with decades of experience, yet they fell into predictable decision-making traps. The intelligence and experience simply made them more confident in their biased choices, not more accurate.

There's another layer: most people learn about biases the way they learn about traffic accidents—as abstract concepts that "happen to other people." The real breakthrough comes when you identify which specific biases actually shape *your* decisions in *your* domain.

The difference between knowing about bias and being protected from it lies in applying that knowledge to your actual decision-making processes.
