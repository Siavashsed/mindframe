## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is decision fatigue real, or is the idea that willpower runs out a myth?

Short answer: decision fatigue is real as an experience, but the popular explanation, that willpower is a tank that drains until you're empty, is mostly wrong.

I'm a psychology student at York University, and I ran into this personally during midterms week last year. I was mentally worn out and couldn't pick something as simple as dinner. The common explanation I'd always heard was 'ego depletion': every act of self-control uses up a limited resource, so eventually you have nothing left.

The problem is that this model hasn't held up well. Large, coordinated replication attempts of the classic ego depletion experiments found much smaller effects than the original studies, and some found essentially none. The idea that willpower is literally fuelled by something like glucose has also been heavily challenged.

What the evidence supports better is a motivation-based account. Your brain is constantly estimating whether continued effort is worth the cost. After a long stretch of demanding, repetitive or unrewarding work, the balance shifts, and you become more impulsive, more avoidant, and more likely to choose the easy option. Your decisions get worse not because something ran out, but because your brain has reassessed what's worth bothering with.

This has practical consequences:

1. Rest helps, but so does changing the task. Variety and meaning can restore effort in ways sleep alone can't.
2. Reduce trivial decisions (routines, defaults) so your effort goes to choices that matter.
3. Make the reward of the task visible. Perceived value drives persistence.
4. Don't treat tiredness as proof you're broken. It's information about your priorities and workload.
