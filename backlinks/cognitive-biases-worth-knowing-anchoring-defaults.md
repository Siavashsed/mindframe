## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which cognitive biases actually affect everyday decisions, and are they all equally well supported by research?

Not all cognitive biases are equal, either in how much they affect your life or in how well the evidence holds up. There are well over a hundred named biases, and most of them are labels for narrow lab effects that will never touch a real decision. A few, though, quietly steer your money, your opinions and your sense of who is reasonable.

The one I would put first is anchoring. When you meet a number before making a judgment, that number pulls your estimate toward it, even when it is arbitrary. I learned this the embarrassing way. I was buying a used ergonomic chair, and the seller asked $900. I haggled him down to $620 and felt clever, but I had never independently decided what the chair was worth. His opening figure had defined 'fair' for me. The fix is simple but takes discipline: form your own estimate before you see their number.

Second are defaults. People tend to stick with whatever option is pre-selected, whether that is a retirement plan enrollment, an organ donor registration or a subscription renewal. The effect is strong because changing the default takes effort, and effort is something we conserve.

Social comparison is another that matters. We judge whether our opinions, income or choices are 'normal' by looking at the people nearest to us, and that circle is usually much narrower than the real population.

Finally, it is worth being honest that some very famous findings in pop psychology have a weaker evidence base than their reputation suggests. Replication problems have hit parts of this field, so it pays to ask what a claim is actually built on.

I go through four biases worth knowing and one famous one to doubt in more detail in this piece, if you want additional reading: https://siavashsed.github.io/mindframe/cognitive-biases-worth-knowing-anchoring-defaults/

The broader point is that knowing the name of a bias rarely protects you. Changing the setup of the decision does.
