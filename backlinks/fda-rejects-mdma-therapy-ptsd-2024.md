I've followed the MDMA-assisted therapy research for a few years and really wanted it to succeed, so the August 2024 FDA rejection stung at first. Then I went through the June 4 advisory committee materials, and I came away thinking the panel's 2 to 9 vote on effectiveness was justified.

For me the biggest issue was functional unblinding. MDMA's effects are obvious, so most participants could tell whether they got the drug or the placebo. The outcome was a clinician-rated symptom score, and the therapy itself depends heavily on expectation and rapport. None of this means MDMA doesn't work. It means these trials couldn't show it cleanly enough, and people with PTSD deserve to have that bar met.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why did the FDA reject MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD?

I'm a psychology student who has followed this research closely, and I was hoping for approval. After going through the record, though, I think the decision was defensible.

On August 9, 2024, the FDA sent Lykos Therapeutics a complete response letter. It declined to approve MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD and asked for another Phase 3 trial. Before that, at a June 4 advisory committee meeting, the panel voted 9 to 2 that the evidence did not show the treatment was effective and 10 to 1 that its benefits did not outweigh its risks.

The main reasons:

1. Functional unblinding. MDMA's effects are unmistakable, so most participants correctly guessed whether they got the drug. Expectation and the therapeutic relationship already matter a lot in this kind of treatment, so it's hard to know how much of the improvement came from the compound itself.

2. Incomplete safety data. Reviewers found that positive effects such as euphoria were not consistently recorded as adverse events, and that matters when you assess abuse potential. Cardiovascular and liver risks also needed more data.

3. Concerns about how the trials were run. A documented case of therapist misconduct in an earlier study, plus questions about how some data were collected, weakened confidence in the overall package.

4. Durability. There was limited evidence on how long the benefits lasted.

It's worth being clear about what the rejection does not say. It does not say MDMA is useless for PTSD. It says these trials could not prove it to the standard we apply to every other drug, and people living with PTSD deserve that standard.
