**Hook:** I kept a 41-day brain training streak. My score went up. My memory did not.

Adult neuroplasticity is real, and it gets oversold all the time. Your brain keeps rewiring itself for life, but it rewires around what you actually practice, not around a general idea of 'getting smarter.'

That's why brain training apps disappoint. People reliably get better at the trained game. The gains rarely carry over to the unrelated, everyday thinking we care about at work.

The research on real change points somewhere else: sustained, demanding, specific practice. Learning a language, an instrument, a new technical skill, or a hard part of your own job. The skill you repeat under real difficulty is the one that grows.

The practical takeaway for professionals is simple. Pick the capability you want, then practice that capability directly. Skip the shortcuts that promise a general upgrade.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Do brain training games actually improve your memory and intelligence as an adult?

Mostly no, at least not the way the ads suggest. That doesn't mean your adult brain has stopped changing. It means the change is far more specific than people expect.

I'm a psychology student, and I tested this on myself before I read the research properly. In the winter of 2024 I kept a 41-day streak on a brain training app, about 15 minutes every morning on the bus to campus. My score on the memory game rose steadily. In daily life I noticed no difference at all. I still forgot names and still lost my place in dense readings.

The literature tells the same story. A large 2016 review by Simons and colleagues in Psychological Science in the Public Interest found strong evidence that people improve on the tasks they train. The evidence for near transfer to closely related tasks was weaker, and the evidence for far transfer to general cognition like reasoning, everyday memory or school performance was thin. Getting faster at matching colored shapes mostly makes you faster at matching colored shapes.

The interesting part is that adult plasticity is genuinely strong when the practice is real. Studies have found measurable changes in gray matter in adults learning to juggle, and in the hippocampus of London taxi drivers who spend years memorizing the city. The common thread is sustained, effortful practice of a specific, meaningful skill.

So if you want a better memory for something, practice remembering that thing. If you want sharper thinking in your field, do harder work in your field. Sleep, exercise and learning genuinely new skills also have better support than app streaks.
