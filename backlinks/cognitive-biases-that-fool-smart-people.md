## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do smart people still fall for cognitive biases, even when they know about them?

Short answer: intelligence helps you reason, but it does not help you notice when your reasoning has gone wrong. Those are different skills.

The clearest evidence comes from research on the bias blind spot. Emily Pronin and her colleagues found that people easily see biases in other people's judgments but rate themselves as less biased than average. Later work by Richard West and colleagues found that higher cognitive ability did not reduce this blind spot, and in some measures it was slightly larger. Smart people are often better at building convincing justifications for conclusions they already reached.

There is also a mechanical reason. Most biases work below conscious awareness. Knowing the definition of anchoring does not stop the first number you hear from pulling your estimate toward it. The bias happens before your knowledge of it can step in.

I have my own humbling example. As a psychology student at York, I once told my study group our research methods proposal would take "two evenings, tops." It took eleven days. I had literally just studied the planning fallacy. Knowing the term did nothing, because in the moment my estimate felt accurate, not biased.

What works better than knowledge is structure:

1. Use outside data. Estimate how long a task will take from how long similar tasks actually took, not from how this one feels.
2. Argue the other side. Before you commit to a belief, write down the strongest case against it.
3. Separate past costs from future value. Ask whether you would start this today if you had not already invested in it.
4. Treat certainty as a signal. A strong feeling of objectivity is a cue to slow down.

You do not need to memorize 180 biases. A handful of checks aimed at the ones that shape your weekly decisions will do more. I wrote a longer breakdown of those few here if it is useful: https://siavashsed.github.io/mindframe/cognitive-biases-that-fool-smart-people/
